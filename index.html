FlowVLA

Enabling World Dynamics Understanding
through Pluggable 3D Flow Learning

Junhyeong Go1Mingyu Jung1Woobin Im2Jongbin Ryu1
1 Ajou University2 Samsung Electronics
GitHub Code ↗Coming soon BibTeX

1 Abstract

Recent advances in 3D reconstruction enable the extraction of temporally consistent 3D motion from RGB observations; however, current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models still predominantly rely on 2D image-text priors. Therefore, they do not accurately represent the fundamental dynamics of the 3D world associated with physical actions. This over-reliance on static 2D visual cues limits the capacity of VLA models in achieving robust robot manipulation. To address these limitations, we introduce FlowVLA, a reconstruction-guided, pluggable 3D flow learning framework aimed at empowering VLA models with an inherent understanding of action-relevant dynamics. Instead of requiring explicit 3D sensor inputs or altering the inference-time architecture, our method is designed to utilize temporally aligned 3D flow only during training. Our method allows the model to learn the dynamic evolution of the physical scene while preserving the computational efficiency of conventional 2D VLA backbones. To bridge scene-level dynamics with robotic manipulation, we propose a modular 3D flow learning framework defined by a dual-level flow supervision design. Specifically, point-level flow guides the model to forecast the future displacements of task-critical 3D points, while TCP-level flow aggregates these motions to align with the robot’s Tool-Center-Point (TCP) trajectories. This dual-level flow supervision encourages the model to focus on semantically meaningful dynamic regions, effectively coupling scene-level motion with precise control signals. Extensive evaluations across simulation and real-world experiments demonstrate that FlowVLA consistently improves manipulation performance, zero-shot robustness, and optimizes data efficiency. FlowVLA adds only lightweight auxiliary heads during training and discards them at inference. Therefore, FlowVLA introduces no additional 3D sensor inputs, architectural modifications, or inference-time computation.

2 Method

We adopt VDPM, a model intended to recover temporally consistent 3D point trajectories from RGB observations, to obtain a 3D motion signal without any other sensors. Here, “world dynamics” denotes manipulation-relevant 3D scene motion. Before constructing flow targets, all 3D points are expressed in the robot base coordinate to ensure that the reconstruction-derived point trajectories are directly comparable with robot motion. We prioritize the selection of dynamically informative points according to their motion magnitude.

These targets supervise the VLA with a dual-level scene-to-control objective: the action latent is encouraged by point-level flow to encode dense 3D scene motion, while motion cues are distilled into a control-oriented representation aligned with end-effector displacement by TCP-level flow. The final objective combines the action loss with the auxiliary flow losses. Critically, all auxiliary heads and 3D geometric inputs are discarded at inference. The quality of the auxiliary supervision depends on the reconstruction model, and magnitude-based point selection does not explicitly guarantee task relevance.

For visual clarity, we display all reconstructed points in this analysis, whereas FlowVLA uses only the top-K dynamically informative points selected by the flow magnitude during training. 5 Analysis

5-a Ablation Studies

Removing either TCP-level flow or point-level flow degrades performance, demonstrating that FlowVLA benefits from both scene-level motion grounding and end-effector-aligned control supervision.

5-b TCP-Level Aggregation Weight Visualization

We further visualize the learned aggregation weights of the TCP-level branch to examine how FlowVLA transfers scene-level 3D motion cues into control-relevant dynamics. The TCP-level aggregation module further refines these scene-level motion cues by assigning larger weights to points around the robot TCP and object–robot interaction region.

Stack Cups · Four synchronized views

The weighted view shows the 512 highest-weight points. TCP trajectory: ground truth in black, prediction in red.

5-c 3D Object Points

Across 100 random examples, the object-point fraction rises from 29.1% among all reconstructed points to 84.8% among the 512 points with the highest TCP aggregation weights. Object points include the gripper, robot arm, and other scene objects.
